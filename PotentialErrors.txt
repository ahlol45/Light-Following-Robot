-Weight Distribution (The "Anchor" Effect)
The batter holder was anchored at the back of the breadboard tilting the breadboard like a seesaw and lifting the wheels of the ground lowering its torque and increasing its start up current needed. 
Fix: Make sure to anchor the holder such that it's placed on top of the 2 motors or in the middle of the breadboard.

-Insufficient Starting Torque
While using 6AA batteries it was just fine but when the battery holder's weight was added ontop of the chasis the motors needed a bigger start up torque.
Fix: 6AA batteries were switched to 7AA battery and the gap was joined using a copper wire

-Logic Vs Drive Power Interference
I hooked up 7.5V to both logic (Vcc1) and the drive power (Vcc2) but I kept getting random results (one wheel spinning)
Fix: Stick to the rated voltage per pin of the motor driver and use a voltage regulator to step the voltage down.

*If all errors avoided and still only one wheel is spinning, it's almost always a power issue. Check wether your batteries are drained or not*
